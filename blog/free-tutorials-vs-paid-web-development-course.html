Free Tutorials vs a Paid Web Development Course

There's an enormous amount of free coding material online, and some of it is excellent. So why would anyone pay for a course? Sometimes you shouldn't. Here's how to decide.

By For Real Academy · Published 2026-10-12 · 5 min read

What free resources do well

Documentation, open tutorials and video series are free, current and often written by the people who make the tools. For looking things up, they're hard to beat. If you're disciplined and already know roughly what to learn, free material can take you a long way.

Where free material struggles

  • No path. You have to decide what to learn, in what order, and when you've learned enough.
  • Mismatched pieces. A tutorial from 2019 and one from 2025 may assume different tools, and you won't always notice.
  • No finish line. It's easy to start ten tutorials and complete none.

What you're actually paying for

Not information, which is mostly free, but curation and sequence: someone decided what matters, in what order, and built exercises to match. If that saves you weeks of wandering, it can be worth the price. If you already have a clear plan, it may not be.

A sensible way to decide

  1. Spend a weekend on free material. Note whether you stay on track.
  2. If you finish something, keep going free. If you stall at "what next?", a structured course may help.
  3. Before paying, check the syllabus, what you'll build, what support exists and the refund policy.

Our take: free and paid aren't opposites. Use free documentation constantly, and pay for a course only if the structure is what you're missing.
