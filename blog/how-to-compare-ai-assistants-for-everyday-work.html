ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini: How to Compare AI Assistants

Which AI assistant is best? The honest answer is: it depends on your tasks, and the answer changes every few months. Instead of declaring a winner that will be outdated soon, here's a method you can reuse whenever a new model appears.

By For Real Academy · Published 2026-10-12 · 7 min read

Why rankings go stale

AI assistants are updated constantly. Benchmarks measure narrow things, and a model that tops a chart may be mediocre at the work you do. The only comparison that matters is how each one performs on your tasks.

What to compare

CriterionWhat to check
Accuracy on your tasksDoes it get your real work right? Check facts it states against sources you trust
Honesty about uncertaintyDoes it say "I don't know" or invent plausible answers?
Long documentsCan you paste in a long report or contract and get a faithful summary? Spot-check it
Instruction followingDoes it respect format, length and tone constraints you set?
SourcesCan it show where information came from, and do those sources really say that?
PrivacyWhat happens to your data? Look at the provider's data and training settings before pasting anything sensitive
Cost and limitsWhat does the free tier allow, and what do paid plans cost today?
Fit with your toolsDoes it integrate with the apps and files you already use?

A five-prompt test you can run in 20 minutes

  1. A real task from last week. Something you already know the right answer to.
  2. A summary. Paste in a document you know well and check what it gets wrong or leaves out.
  3. A fact check. Ask about something obscure you can verify. Notice whether it hedges or bluffs.
  4. A constrained rewrite. "Rewrite this in 80 words, friendly but professional, no jargon."
  5. A follow-up. Ask it to revise its answer. Does it use the feedback or restart?

Run the same prompts, word for word, in each assistant you're considering. Write down a score for each criterion. You'll learn more from this than from any article, including this one.

Don't forget the human part

The biggest difference between good and poor results is usually how you ask, not which tool you use. Clear context, examples and constraints improve results with every assistant. And whichever you pick, you stay responsible for checking the output.

Our take: pick two assistants, run the five-prompt test, and re-run it every few months. Keep whichever suits your work, and don't be afraid to use more than one.
